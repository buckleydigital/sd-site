Why CEC Accreditation Matters

The Clean Energy Council (CEC) is Australia's peak solar industry body. CEC accreditation is not just an industry badge — it is a practical requirement for homeowners seeking to access federal government rebates on solar and battery storage.

  • Access to rebates: Only CEC-accredited installers can process the federal Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) rebate for solar systems. Without accreditation, your system may not qualify.
  • Trained and qualified: CEC members must hold the relevant electrical and solar installation licences and undertake ongoing training to maintain accreditation.
  • Code of conduct: Accredited installers are bound by a CEC code of conduct covering quality, safety, and customer treatment.
  • Vetted and insured: All installers in the ReclaimPower network hold CEC accreditation and appropriate insurance coverage.

When you get a quote through ReclaimPower, you can be confident your installer meets these minimum standards. For more on available rebates, visit our Battery Rebates page.

How does ReclaimPower match homeowners with installers?
ReclaimPower takes your postcode and details, then connects you with one pre-vetted CEC-accredited local installer in your area. The service is completely free and obligation-free — you receive a tailored quote with no sales pressure and no obligation to proceed.
What does CEC accreditation mean?
CEC (Clean Energy Council) accreditation means an installer has met the standards required by Australia's peak solar industry body. CEC accreditation is required for homeowners to access federal government solar and battery rebates, and ensures the installer is trained, qualified, and bound by a professional code of conduct.
