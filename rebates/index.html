Solar & Battery Rebates Australia 2026 —
Every State Explained

Two major federal programs are currently available to all eligible Australian homeowners: the long-standing Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) solar rebate and the new Cheaper Home Batteries Program launched in 2025. On top of these federal programs, several states and territories offer additional incentives that can significantly reduce your upfront costs.

Federal Solar Rebate — The STC System

The Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) scheme is Australia's primary federal mechanism for reducing the upfront cost of residential solar installations. When you install a solar system with a CEC-accredited installer, your installer creates and surrenders STCs on your behalf. The monetary value of those certificates is then passed on to you as a discount on the purchase price of your system — meaning the rebate is applied at the point of sale and you never need to deal with the rebate process yourself.

The number of STCs your system generates depends on two factors: the size (capacity) of your solar system and your geographic location zone, which reflects how much solar energy the system is expected to generate over its lifetime. Systems installed in sunnier regions (such as Queensland) generate more STCs and therefore attract a larger discount than those in cooler or cloudier climates.

For a typical 6.6kW solar system installed in most Australian capital cities, the STC discount is approximately $2,500 to $5,000 (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). Your CEC-accredited installer handles all STC paperwork on your behalf and the discount is simply reflected in your final quoted price. The STC scheme continues to operate while federal government support remains in place — always verify the current STC value with your installer or at energy.gov.au.

⚠

STC Value Fluctuates

The value of STCs changes based on market trading. Your installer will confirm the exact discount applicable at the time of your quote. Always get a written quote that itemises the STC discount separately.

Federal Battery Rebate — Cheaper Home Batteries Program

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program is a $7.2 billion initiative launched in 2025 and funded through to 2030. It represents the most significant home battery incentive ever offered at a national level in Australia and is designed to accelerate the uptake of home battery storage across the country.

The program provides approximately 30% off the installed cost of an eligible home battery system (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). This means a battery system that would otherwise cost $10,000 installed could be available for approximately $7,000 after the rebate is applied — though exact amounts depend on your system, installer, and current program values.

To access the Cheaper Home Batteries Program, you must use a CEC-accredited installer and the battery model you install must be on the government's list of approved, eligible products. The rebate is applied at the point of purchase — your installer facilitates the process on your behalf. This federal program can stack with certain state-level programs, meaning eligible homeowners in some states may receive both federal and state incentives simultaneously.

🔋

Stacks With State Programs

In Western Australia and the ACT, the federal battery rebate can stack with state-level loan schemes. In Victoria, the federal battery rebate and the Solar Homes solar rebate may both apply. Ask your installer about your specific entitlements.

State-by-State Overview

While the federal programs are available to all eligible Australian homeowners regardless of state, each state and territory has its own additional programs, policies, and feed-in tariff arrangements. Click through to your state for a detailed guide.

Queensland

Queensland homeowners benefit from the federal STC solar rebate and the Cheaper Home Batteries Program. As of 2026, there is no Queensland-specific state battery rebate stacking on top of the federal program. However, Queensland's exceptional sunshine hours mean solar and battery systems generate strong returns. Feed-in tariffs vary by retailer — compare retailers for the best available rate.

New South Wales

NSW homeowners access both federal programs. Some Virtual Power Plant (VPP) providers operating in NSW offer additional bill credits to battery participants — however these are private arrangements, not government-guaranteed grants, and vary significantly by provider. Discuss VPP eligibility with your installer.

Victoria

Victoria's Solar Homes Program offers eligible households up to $1,400 towards a solar panel installation (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). The program is income and property-value tested, and availability varies. The federal battery rebate applies separately. Verify current program status at solar.vic.gov.au.

Western Australia

Synergy customers in WA may be eligible for an interest-free loan of up to $10,000 to assist with the purchase and installation of a home battery system (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). This is a loan, not a grant — it must be repaid. This can be combined with the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program for eligible customers. Verify at synergy.net.au.

South Australia

South Australia's state Home Battery Scheme ended in 2021. SA homeowners now rely primarily on the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program. SA has one of the highest solar penetration rates in Australia and a battery is particularly valuable given SA's electricity tariff structure. Check energy.sa.gov.au for any new state programs.

Australian Capital Territory

The ACT Sustainable Household Scheme offers eligible ACT households a zero-interest loan of up to $15,000 for sustainable upgrades including solar, batteries, and electric vehicles (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). This is a loan — it must be repaid. Combined with the federal battery rebate, eligible ACT homeowners may access significant combined support. Verify at climatechoices.act.gov.au.

General Eligibility Requirements

While specific eligibility criteria vary by program, most Australian solar and battery rebate programs share a set of common requirements that all applicants must meet.

  • Property owner: You must own the property where the system is being installed. Renters generally cannot access these programs directly, though some landlord-specific programs exist in certain states.
  • CEC-accredited installer: You must use a Clean Energy Council (CEC) accredited installer for both the STC solar rebate and the Cheaper Home Batteries Program. This is non-negotiable — using a non-accredited installer disqualifies your claim.
  • Eligible property: The property must be a residential property. Some programs have additional property value caps or location restrictions.
  • Eligible product: For the battery rebate, the battery model you install must appear on the government's approved product list. Your installer will confirm eligibility.
  • Income testing: Some state programs (such as Victoria's Solar Homes Program) apply income or means testing. Check state-specific pages for details.

Always verify current eligibility criteria directly with your installer or at energy.gov.au before making any financial decisions. Program criteria can change without notice.
