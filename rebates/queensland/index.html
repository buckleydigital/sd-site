Solar & Battery Rebates Queensland 2026 — Complete Guide

Queensland's combination of high sunshine hours and the federal rebate programs makes 2026 an excellent time for homeowners to consider solar and battery storage. With some of the best solar resources in the country, Queenslanders consistently see strong returns on well-sized solar systems — and adding a battery extends those savings into the evening hours.

Federal Solar Rebate — How STCs Work in Queensland

The Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) scheme is the primary federal mechanism for reducing the upfront cost of residential solar installations across Australia, including Queensland. When you install a solar system with a CEC-accredited installer, your installer creates and surrenders STCs on your behalf, and the value of those certificates is passed directly to you as a discount on your purchase price. The rebate is applied at the point of sale — you pay the reduced price, and your installer handles all the paperwork.

The number of STCs your system generates depends on its size (capacity in kilowatts) and your geographic location zone. Queensland falls within solar zones that attract higher STC quantities than southern states, meaning the dollar value of the STC discount is typically greater for Queensland homeowners. For a typical 6.6kW solar system installed in most Queensland postcodes, the STC discount is approximately $2,500 to $5,000 (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au).

It is important to understand that the STC value fluctuates based on certificate market prices, which change regularly. Your installer will confirm the exact discount applicable at the time of your quote. Always get a written quote that clearly itemises the STC rebate as a separate line item so you know exactly what discount has been applied.

Queensland Solar Zone Advantage

Queensland's location means solar systems generate more STCs than equivalent systems installed in southern states. This translates to a larger point-of-sale solar discount for Queensland homeowners compared with many other regions. Verify current values with your CEC-accredited installer.

The STC scheme continues to operate while federal government support remains in place. Verify the current STC value with your installer or directly at energy.gov.au before finalising your purchase decision.

Federal Battery Rebate — Cheaper Home Batteries Program

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program launched in 2025 and is funded through to 2030. With a total program value of $7.2 billion, it represents the largest home battery incentive ever offered at a national level in Australia. Queensland homeowners are fully eligible for this program alongside their counterparts in every other Australian state and territory.

The program provides approximately 30% off the installed cost of an eligible home battery system (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). To give a practical illustration: a battery system with a total installed cost of $10,000 before the rebate may be available for approximately $7,000 after the federal discount is applied (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). The exact rebate amount depends on the specific battery model, system configuration, and current program values at the time of installation.

To access the Cheaper Home Batteries Program in Queensland, you must use a CEC-accredited installer and select a battery model that appears on the government's approved product list. Your installer will confirm which batteries are eligible and apply the rebate at the point of installation on your behalf. You do not need to lodge any application yourself — the process is handled entirely by your accredited installer.

No State Stacking in Queensland

Unlike WA and the ACT, Queensland does not currently have a state-level program that stacks on top of the federal battery rebate. However, the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program alone provides significant savings. Verify current program details at energy.gov.au.

Queensland State Programs

As of 2026, Queensland does not have a state-specific home battery rebate or solar rebate that stacks on top of the federal programs. Queensland homeowners rely on the federal STC solar rebate and the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program as their primary incentive mechanisms.

The Queensland Government has historically offered various energy assistance programs, particularly aimed at low-income households and concession card holders. It is always worth checking the Energex website and the Queensland Government's energy page for any new announcements, as state-level programs can be introduced or updated at any time.

Feed-in Tariffs in Queensland

Queensland does not set a mandated minimum feed-in tariff rate. Instead, rates are negotiated between homeowners and their electricity retailer and typically range from approximately 6 to 12 cents per kilowatt-hour (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). The Queensland Competition Authority publishes a reference price, but actual rates vary. It is strongly recommended to compare electricity retailers when installing solar, as the feed-in tariff rate will meaningfully affect your bill savings and payback period.

A home battery further reduces the impact of a low feed-in tariff, because instead of exporting surplus solar at a low rate, you can store that energy and use it yourself in the evening — maximising the value of every kilowatt-hour your system generates.

Eligibility Criteria for Queensland Homeowners

To access the available federal solar and battery rebates in Queensland, the following general eligibility requirements apply:

  • Property owner: You must own the residential property where the system is being installed. Renters are generally ineligible for these programs unless specific landlord arrangements apply.
  • CEC-accredited installer: You must use a Clean Energy Council (CEC) accredited installer. This is a strict requirement for both the STC solar rebate and the Cheaper Home Batteries Program. ReclaimPower only connects homeowners with CEC-accredited installers.
  • Eligible property: The property must be a residential dwelling. Some programs have additional criteria relating to property type or postcode.
  • Eligible battery model: For the battery rebate, the battery you install must be on the government's approved product list. Your installer will confirm which models qualify.
  • New installation: Rebates generally apply to new installations, not replacements of existing systems in all cases — confirm with your installer.

Always verify current eligibility criteria with your installer or directly at energy.gov.au before committing to a purchase. Eligibility rules can change.

Is Solar and Battery Worth It in Queensland?

Queensland consistently ranks as one of Australia's best states for solar energy generation, thanks to its high annual sunshine hours and favourable climate. The combination of abundant sunshine and the available federal rebates means Queensland homeowners can achieve some of the strongest financial returns from solar and battery installations in the country.
