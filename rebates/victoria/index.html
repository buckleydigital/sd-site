🏭 Victoria Rebates 2026

Solar & Battery Rebates Victoria 2026 — Complete Guide

Victoria offers a combination of state and federal rebate programs that can make solar and battery installations particularly attractive for eligible homeowners. The Victorian Solar Homes Program provides a solar panel rebate on top of the federal STC scheme, while the Cheaper Home Batteries Program applies separately to eligible battery systems.

Federal Solar Rebate — How STCs Work in Victoria

The Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) scheme is the primary federal mechanism for reducing the upfront cost of residential solar installations. When you install solar with a CEC-accredited installer in Victoria, your installer creates and surrenders STCs on your behalf, and the certificate value is passed to you as a direct point-of-sale discount — no paperwork required from you.

The STC discount for Victorian households depends on your system size and geographic zone. Victoria's climate and sunshine hours mean STC quantities are generally somewhat lower than in Queensland or Western Australia, but still provide a meaningful reduction in upfront cost. For a typical 6.6kW solar system in most Victorian postcodes, the STC discount represents a significant contribution to overall system cost savings (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au).

The STC value fluctuates based on certificate market prices. Your installer will confirm the exact discount at the time of your quote. Always verify the current STC situation at energy.gov.au and ensure your written quote clearly itemises the STC rebate separately.

Victorian Solar Homes Program

The Victorian Solar Homes Program is a state government initiative designed to help eligible Victorian households access solar panel installations at a reduced cost. The program offers eligible households a solar panel rebate of up to $1,400 (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au) that stacks on top of the federal STC discount, further reducing the upfront cost of a solar installation.

Eligibility Requirements

The Victorian Solar Homes Program is income and property-value tested. Key eligibility requirements typically include:

  • Combined household taxable income below the program threshold (verify at solar.vic.gov.au)
  • Property value below the program cap (verify at solar.vic.gov.au)
  • The property must not have previously received a Solar Homes Program rebate
  • A CEC-accredited installer must be used
  • The installation must meet program specifications
Important: Victorian Solar Homes Program availability and waitlist status changes regularly. Rebates may be allocated in batches and a waitlist may apply. Always verify current program status, rebate amounts, and eligibility criteria at solar.vic.gov.au before making any decisions.

Applying for the Victorian Solar Homes Program

Applications for the Victorian Solar Homes Program are made through the Solar Victoria website at solar.vic.gov.au. Your CEC-accredited installer will guide you through the application process and ensure all documentation is submitted correctly. ReclaimPower can match you with a local CEC-accredited installer who is familiar with the Victorian Solar Homes Program process.

Federal Battery Rebate — Cheaper Home Batteries Program

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program launched in 2025 and is funded through to 2030 with a total budget of $7.2 billion. It is Australia's largest-ever national home battery incentive and is available to all eligible Victorian homeowners.

The program provides approximately 30% off the installed cost of an eligible home battery system (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au). A battery system with a total installed cost of $10,000 may therefore be available for approximately $7,000 after the rebate is applied (figures approximate — verify current amounts at energy.gov.au), though the exact amount depends on the battery model and current program values.

The Victorian Solar Homes solar rebate (up to $1,400) applies to the solar panel component of your installation, while the federal battery rebate applies separately to the battery system. If you are installing both solar and a battery simultaneously, you may be eligible for both programs — your CEC-accredited installer can confirm eligibility and help you structure the installation to maximise available incentives. Always verify current stacking rules at solar.vic.gov.au and energy.gov.au.

Potential to Stack Both Programs

Victorian homeowners installing both solar and a battery may be eligible for the Victorian Solar Homes solar rebate AND the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program simultaneously. Your CEC-accredited installer can confirm eligibility for both programs.

Eligibility Criteria for Victorian Homeowners

To access Victoria's available solar and battery rebate programs, the following general requirements apply:

  • Property owner: You must own the residential property where the system is being installed.
  • CEC-accredited installer: You must use a Clean Energy Council (CEC) accredited installer for both state and federal programs. ReclaimPower only connects Victorian homeowners with CEC-accredited installers.
  • Income testing (Solar Homes): The Victorian Solar Homes Program is income and property-value tested. Verify current thresholds at solar.vic.gov.au.
  • No previous Solar Homes rebate: Properties that have previously received a Victorian Solar Homes rebate may not be eligible again. Check the program rules.
  • Eligible battery model: For the federal battery rebate, the battery must appear on the government's approved product list.

Always verify current eligibility criteria at solar.vic.gov.au and energy.gov.au before committing to any purchase.

Is Solar and Battery Worth It in Victoria?

Victoria has significant solar potential despite its more temperate climate compared with northern states. Melbourne and regional Victoria receive sufficient sunshine to make solar a financially sound investment, particularly given rising electricity prices. The Victorian Solar Homes rebate, stacked with the federal STC discount, can significantly reduce the upfront cost of a solar installation for eligible households.

Adding a home battery allows Victorian households to store surplus solar energy generated during the day for use in the evening — reducing reliance on grid power during peak tariff periods. Given the electricity tariff structure in Victoria, this can meaningfully accelerate your return on investment.
